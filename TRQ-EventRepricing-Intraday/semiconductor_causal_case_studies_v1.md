# Semiconductor Causal Event Case Studies v1

## Purpose

Semiconductor event-day research must not be reduced to `EPS beat + guidance beat + gap size`. The economic meaning of an earnings or demand shock depends on where a company sits in the value chain, how it monetizes design wins, how quickly demand becomes recognized revenue and free cash flow, how much pricing power it retains, what bottlenecks limit supply, what customers are funding the demand, and what the market already expected.

This file applies the same causal standard used in the biotech case studies, but with a semiconductor-specific ontology. The intraday state engine remains separate and cannot infer business quality from the stock path.

Historical judgments are point-in-time. Later outcomes may validate the thesis but must not be back-filled into the event prior.

---

# Case 1 — ARM / Q3 FYE24 results / 2024-02-07 to 2024-02-08

## 1. The real investment question

The event was not simply `revenue $824m above guidance -> AI stock rises`.

The economically relevant question was whether Arm's post-IPO business model was beginning to monetize several structural transitions at once:

`Armv9 adoption -> higher royalty rate per chip`

`cloud/automotive share gain -> new royalty streams`

`AI demand -> more advanced licenses/design wins`

`design wins -> future chip shipments -> future royalties`

The event therefore updated both current earnings and the expected slope of the royalty stream.

## 2. Business model: license first, royalty later

Arm does not primarily manufacture chips. Customers pay license fees to access Arm IP and develop processors; once those processors are manufactured and shipped, Arm receives per-unit royalties on substantially all Arm-based chips.

This produces a two-stage economic model:

`architecture/IP adoption -> license revenue and design wins -> customer development lag -> chip shipment volume/ASP -> royalty revenue`

A licensing surge can therefore contain information about future royalties that is not visible in the current quarter's royalty revenue. Conversely, license revenue can be lumpy, and a design win is not the same as recognized future royalty dollars.

## 3. Why Armv9 mattered more than simple unit growth

Arm explicitly stated that royalty rates for Armv9 products were typically at least double the rates for equivalent Armv8 products. That means the royalty opportunity is not just `more chips`; it is a mix/architecture monetization story.

A smartphone market recovery can raise unit shipments, while a transition to Armv9 can simultaneously raise royalty economics per unit. Cloud-server and automotive share gains create additional end-market mix effects.

Thus the causal revenue bridge should separate:

- unit/end-market recovery;
- Arm share within each end market;
- architecture mix (v8 vs v9 and future generations);
- royalty rate/ASP economics;
- licensing/design-win pipeline;
- lag from license to volume shipment.

## 4. What the Q3 event actually added

Arm reported Q3 FYE24 revenue of $824 million versus prior guidance of $720-$800 million. Royalty revenue was $470 million, up 11% year over year; license and other revenue was $354 million, up 18%. Non-GAAP operating margin was 41%.

More important than the quarter alone, Arm raised FY24 revenue guidance from $2.960-$3.080 billion to $3.155-$3.205 billion and guided Q4 revenue to $850-$900 million.

Management attributed record royalty revenue to Armv9 adoption, cloud-server and automotive share gains, and smartphone recovery, while licensing benefited from AI-driven demand for new technology.

The event therefore combined:

1. current-quarter beat;
2. raised near-term earnings/revenue path;
3. evidence that higher-monetization architecture was moving into shipments;
4. evidence that AI was affecting the licensing pipeline across cloud and edge.

## 5. Market structure mattered, but is not business value

Arm had only recently returned to public markets. Its September 2023 IPO sold 102.5 million ADSs, while SoftBank retained roughly 90% ownership. The prospectus stated SoftBank was expected to own about 90.6% after the offering; a later filing showed approximately 89.8% beneficial ownership.

That relatively small public float can amplify the stock response to a large demand shock because fewer freely traded shares absorb incremental demand. This is a **price-formation/positioning variable**, not a fundamental reason to increase enterprise value.

The strategy must therefore distinguish:

`fundamental repricing` from `float/positioning amplification`.

## 6. Macro and semiconductor-cycle context

On January 31, 2024 the Federal Reserve kept the funds-rate range at 5.25%-5.50% and said it did not expect rate cuts until it had greater confidence inflation was moving sustainably toward 2%. This was still a relatively tight discount-rate environment.

Arm also described the broader semiconductor market as recovering, particularly smartphones, while noting inventory correction in IoT/embedded and some automotive-microcontroller weakness.

Therefore the February move was not well explained by broad monetary easing. The main driver was company-specific re-underwriting of AI licensing, v9 monetization and future royalty growth, with a recovering semiconductor cycle as a secondary tailwind.

## 7. Why the equity response could be much larger than the earnings beat

Contemporaneous reporting showed Arm opening roughly 58% higher and trading above $122, around a $120 billion valuation.

The causal explanation is broader than one quarter's earnings surprise:

- Armv9 improved the monetization rate of an already enormous installed architecture base;
- license revenue contained forward information about future design wins and royalties;
- AI demand broadened the perceived end-market runway beyond smartphones;
- cloud/automotive share gains suggested structural mix improvement;
- raised annual guidance forced near-term estimates higher;
- low public float likely amplified price discovery.

The last item explains elasticity, not intrinsic value. A robust strategy must not confuse the two.

## 8. What remained uncertain

- how quickly AI-related license activity would convert into production royalties;
- sustainability and cyclicality of license revenue;
- customer bargaining power and architecture competition (including x86 and RISC-V);
- actual cloud-server share gains and royalty economics;
- smartphone recovery durability;
- valuation after a very large post-IPO rerating;
- SoftBank ownership/float dynamics.

## 9. Causal prior

**Prior:** strong positive earnings/guidance confirmation with structural monetization evidence; high price elasticity due both to fundamental duration and public-float structure.

**Not equivalent to:** `low float + AI headline -> buy`, or proof that every intraday extension reflects additional fundamental information.

# Case 2 — NVDA / Q1 FY2024 and Q2 guide / 2023-05-24 to 2023-05-25

## 1. The real investment question

The event was not simply `Q2 revenue guidance $11B > consensus`.

The decisive question was whether generative AI had moved from a promising technology narrative into an immediate hyperscale capital-spending demand shock that NVIDIA could actually capture through a vertically reinforced platform.

The causal chain was:

`generative AI workload explosion -> accelerated-computing capex -> GPU + networking demand -> CUDA/platform lock-in -> supply allocation -> revenue -> gross profit/operating leverage -> reinvestment into next platform generation`

The $11 billion guide mattered because it provided a near-term bridge from narrative to reported economics.

## 2. Business model and value-chain position

NVIDIA's value capture was qualitatively different from Arm's. It sold high-value accelerated-computing systems/components and networking products rather than collecting a small royalty on broad chip volume.

Its competitive position combined:

- data-center GPUs/accelerators;
- high-speed networking and systems;
- CUDA and a large developer/software ecosystem;
- platform-level optimization and libraries;
- rapid product-generation cadence.

The economic moat was therefore not a single chip benchmark. Customers were buying access to an acceleration platform with hardware, networking and software dependencies.

## 3. What was known before the event

The AI narrative was not born on May 24. NVIDIA had already discussed accelerated computing and generative AI as major drivers. The market had also seen the emergence of ChatGPT and hyperscaler AI spending.

The pre-event uncertainty was instead:

> How quickly would that enthusiasm turn into shipments and revenue at NVIDIA's scale?

This distinction is critical. A theme can be consensus while the **magnitude and timing of monetization** remain badly underestimated.

## 4. What the earnings event actually changed

NVIDIA reported Q1 revenue of $7.19 billion and record Data Center revenue of $4.28 billion. It then guided Q2 revenue to approximately $11.0 billion.

Management described two simultaneous industry transitions: accelerated computing and generative AI, and said the company was significantly increasing supply to meet surging demand.

The guide implied an abrupt step-up from the immediately preceding revenue base, not a slow multi-year narrative. That forced investors to reconsider:

- hyperscaler/enterprise capex intensity;
- NVIDIA's share of that capex;
- supply availability;
- high-margin mix;
- duration of the data-center transition;
- how much of the world's installed general-purpose data-center infrastructure could migrate to accelerated computing.

## 5. Why supply was part of the thesis

Demand is not revenue if supply cannot ship. NVIDIA explicitly said it was significantly increasing supply. In 2023, advanced accelerators also depended on leading-edge foundry capacity, high-bandwidth memory, advanced packaging, substrates, systems integration and networking.

Therefore a proper causal model needs a bottleneck layer:

`orders/demand -> allocated supply -> manufacturable systems -> shipped revenue`.

This is why later event models should treat supply/packaging/network constraints separately from demand strength.

## 6. Operating leverage and economic capture

NVIDIA's Q1 GAAP gross margin was 64.6%. A huge revenue step-up in a high-gross-margin platform has a nonlinear effect on earnings and cash flow because incremental gross profit can grow much faster than fixed operating costs.

This is fundamentally different from a pure unit-growth story. The equity rerating reflected both a larger revenue base and a much larger prospective profit pool.

## 7. Macro context

The Federal Reserve had raised the funds-rate target to 5.00%-5.25% in early May 2023 amid elevated inflation and banking-system stress. This was not a permissive low-rate growth-stock regime.

Accordingly, the magnitude of NVIDIA's repricing is strong evidence that company-specific estimate revision overwhelmed the adverse discount-rate backdrop. Macro still mattered for valuation multiples, but it does not explain the move.

## 8. Why the day behaved differently from many biotech gap-and-go events

Reuters reported NVIDIA rose 24% on May 25 and added roughly $184 billion in market capitalization, approaching $939 billion.

Yet much of the price discovery occurred overnight and at the open rather than through a second large intraday leg. That is economically plausible because:

- the earnings release contained explicit revenue guidance that sell-side and buy-side models could immediately translate into EPS/FCF estimates;
- NVIDIA was already highly liquid and institutionally followed;
- the company had an existing large revenue/profit base;
- the new information could be rapidly incorporated into valuation models before regular trading.

A biotech topline may leave more uncertainty about commercial translation and therefore generate more prolonged disagreement; a mega-cap earnings guide can be more machine-readable and model-ready.

This is a key lesson for the state engine: **same event quality does not imply same speed of price discovery**.

## 9. What remained uncertain

- supply availability and lead times;
- sustainability of hyperscaler capex;
- cloud customer concentration;
- competition from AMD and custom accelerators;
- export controls/geopolitics, especially China exposure;
- normalization risk after the first AI infrastructure build-out;
- valuation after a giant one-day market-cap increase.

## 10. Causal prior

**Prior:** extreme positive near-term estimate revision and structural platform validation; very high fundamental quality, but regular-session continuation need not be large because much of the information was quantitatively digestible before the open.

**Key lesson:** overnight gap size can reflect rapid, rational model revision rather than an exhausted or overvalued trade.

# Case 3 — AVGO / FY2024 results and AI outlook / 2024-12-12 to 2024-12-13

## 1. The real investment question

The event was not simply `AI revenue +220% -> stock +24%`.

The important question was whether Broadcom had established a durable second major AI-infrastructure profit pool alongside NVIDIA by controlling two scarce pieces of hyperscale architecture:

`custom accelerator silicon (XPU)`

plus

`high-speed Ethernet networking/connectivity`.

At the same time, VMware had transformed Broadcom into a hybrid semiconductor + infrastructure-software cash-flow company. The valuation therefore had to incorporate both AI growth and the quality/durability of the software cash engine.

## 2. Business model: custom silicon is not merchant GPU economics

Broadcom's AI model differs from NVIDIA's merchant accelerator platform. Broadcom works with major hyperscalers on custom accelerators and supplies networking/connectivity products that move data across large AI clusters.

That offers hyperscalers a path to diversify from expensive, supply-constrained merchant GPUs and optimize silicon for internal workloads.

The value chain is therefore:

`hyperscaler workload/capex plan -> custom architecture/design engagement -> tape-out/manufacturing -> cluster deployment -> XPU revenue`

plus

`cluster scale -> Ethernet switching/NIC/connectivity demand`.

This creates attractive co-monetization but also greater customer concentration, design-cycle length and deployment lumpiness than a broad merchant-product model.

## 3. Reported fact versus forward narrative

Broadcom reported FY2024 revenue of $51.6 billion, up 44%. Semiconductor revenue was $30.1 billion, with AI revenue of $12.2 billion, up 220% year over year. Infrastructure software revenue reached $21.5 billion following VMware integration. Adjusted EBITDA was $31.9 billion, and free cash flow excluding restructuring was $21.9 billion.

For Q1 FY2025, management guided revenue to approximately $14.6 billion and adjusted EBITDA to about 66% of revenue.

Those are Reported Fact / Management Guidance objects.

Separately, CEO Hock Tan discussed an AI serviceable revenue opportunity of roughly $60-$90 billion in fiscal 2027 based on deployment plans of three hyperscale customers, while the company was engaging additional hyperscalers.

That $60-$90 billion figure must be stored as **Management Guidance / Market Narrative**, not as booked revenue, backlog or Reported Fact. It depends on customer capex plans, Broadcom share, successful custom designs and future deployment timing.

This distinction is essential for TRQuant's metric ontology.

## 4. VMware changes downside and valuation geometry

Unlike a pure-play AI chip company, Broadcom entered the event with a large infrastructure-software business. VMware contributed materially to FY2024 software revenue and helped support a large recurring cash-flow base.

The resulting structure is:

`AI semiconductor upside` + `infrastructure software cash generation` - `integration/customer-retention risk`.

This can lower the company's dependence on any one semiconductor cycle, but it also means the equity is not a clean proxy for AI accelerator demand.

## 5. Profit capture matters as much as revenue growth

Broadcom's Q4 adjusted EBITDA was 65% of revenue and Q4 free cash flow was $5.482 billion, or 39% of revenue. Those economics show that incremental revenue could translate into large cash generation.

Consequently, the event was not only a TAM expansion narrative. It combined:

- demonstrated AI revenue scale;
- very high cash conversion;
- software integration progress;
- forward visibility into additional hyperscaler programs.

That supports a higher-quality shareholder-value bridge than a simple `TAM x future share` story.

## 6. Customer concentration and custom-chip risk

The upside is also the risk. A small number of hyperscalers can account for a large portion of custom accelerator opportunity. Their capex plans can change, internal projects can be delayed, and customers have substantial bargaining/technical power.

Custom silicon has long design and qualification cycles. Winning an engagement is not the same as recognizing revenue. The model must preserve:

`engagement/design win -> development -> qualification -> production -> deployment -> revenue`.

Broadcom also relies substantially on outsourced semiconductor manufacturing, so foundry, packaging and supply-chain constraints remain relevant.

## 7. Macro and broad-market context

The latest FOMC decision available before the December 12 earnings release was November 7, when the Fed cut the target range 25 bps to 4.50%-4.75%. The Fed said economic activity remained solid, inflation had progressed but was still somewhat elevated, and risks to employment and inflation goals were roughly balanced.

The December 18 rate cut had **not yet occurred** and cannot be used in the December 13 event prior.

Broadcom therefore operated in a more supportive rate environment than NVIDIA's May 2023 event, but the December 13 broad indexes were roughly flat while AVGO surged. The event-day residual was overwhelmingly company-specific.

## 8. Why a mega-cap could still reprice more than 20%

Reuters reported Broadcom's valuation crossed $1 trillion as the shares rose roughly 21% during the session; other market data showed a final gain around 24.4%.

This was possible despite the company's size because investors were not merely revising one quarter. They were repricing:

1. the durability and scale of custom AI accelerators;
2. Ethernet as a second AI-cluster monetization layer;
3. prospective expansion from three hyperscalers and additional customer engagements;
4. VMware's contribution to a large cash-flow base;
5. the probability that Broadcom would be one of the few durable non-NVIDIA AI-infrastructure profit pools.

However, the $60-$90 billion FY2027 opportunity remained forecast-dependent and difficult to verify ex ante. A high-quality event prior can therefore coexist with substantial long-horizon expectation risk.

## 9. What remained uncertain

- conversion of hyperscaler plans into shipments;
- customer concentration and bargaining power;
- custom-silicon competition and customers' internal capabilities;
- advanced manufacturing/packaging capacity;
- Ethernet versus alternative interconnect architectures;
- VMware customer retention, pricing and integration outcomes;
- export controls/geopolitical exposure;
- how much of the 2027 opportunity had already been capitalized into the post-event valuation.

## 10. Causal prior

**Prior:** very strong positive structural AI + cash-flow confirmation, with mature-company economics and high retained cash capture; forward AI opportunity is powerful but must remain classified as management expectation rather than realized economics.

# Cross-case causal comparison

| Dimension | ARM | NVDA | AVGO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core monetization | IP licenses + per-chip royalties | merchant accelerators + networking + software platform | custom accelerators + Ethernet + infrastructure software |
| Demand-to-revenue lag | often long: design win to shipped chips | relatively fast when supply available | custom design/qualification can be long and lumpy |
| AI value capture | architecture/royalty uplift across broad ecosystem | very high dollar content per accelerated system + ecosystem | custom silicon share + cluster networking + software cash flow |
| Key bottleneck | design adoption and future shipment conversion | supply/packaging/HBM/system capacity | hyperscaler deployment + foundry/packaging + customer concentration |
| Event surprise | v9 monetization + AI licenses + raised guide | $11B near-term guide converted AI narrative into immediate revenue | reported $12.2B AI scale + very large 2027 opportunity + VMware cash engine |
| Existing business maturity | mature IP model, newly public equity | large profitable semiconductor platform | very large mature semi/software cash generator |
| Positioning amplifier | unusually low public float under SoftBank control | high liquidity; less float distortion | mega-cap institutional liquidity |
| Macro on event | tight Fed policy | tight Fed policy / banking stress | easing cycle underway |
| Price-discovery implication | float can prolong/amplify rush | large part can clear overnight due model-ready guide | long-duration narrative + current cash facts can support continued disagreement |

# Research rules extracted for EventRepricingIntraday

1. **No universal `earnings surprise score`.** Revenue beats have different value implications across royalty, merchant-product and custom-design models.
2. **Separate demand from monetization.** AI attention is irrelevant unless the company can capture it through pricing, share, shipment, royalties or software economics.
3. **Respect revenue lags.** ARM design wins can take years to become royalties; custom ASIC programs can have long qualification cycles; merchant GPU demand can convert faster if supply exists.
4. **Separate unit growth from economics per unit.** Armv9 royalty-rate uplift and NVIDIA accelerator ASP/mix are different from simple shipment growth.
5. **Model bottlenecks explicitly.** Foundry, HBM, advanced packaging, networking, substrates and qualification can cap revenue despite strong demand.
6. **Software/ecosystem changes durability.** CUDA and VMware are economically different assets, but both can alter switching costs, cash flow and valuation duration.
7. **Customer concentration matters differently by business model.** Hyperscaler dependence is central to custom silicon; broad royalty exposure diversifies unit sources but may still concentrate license customers.
8. **Public float is price formation, not intrinsic value.** ARM's SoftBank ownership can amplify a move but cannot be used as a business-quality input.
9. **Distinguish reported fact, guidance and TAM narrative.** Broadcom's $12.2B FY2024 AI revenue is a fact; the $60-$90B FY2027 opportunity is management expectation.
10. **Event quality and price-discovery speed are separate.** NVIDIA can have an extreme-quality event with modest open-to-close continuation because institutional models absorb explicit guidance before the open.
11. **Macro is an amplifier.** A 5%+ funds rate did not prevent ARM/NVDA repricing when estimate revisions were large; easing can support duration but does not create AI economics.
12. **Use one common intraday state engine only after sector-specific causal review.** Shared microstructure does not imply shared fundamental priors.
