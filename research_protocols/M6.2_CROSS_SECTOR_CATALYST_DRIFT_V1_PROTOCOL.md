# M6.2 — Cross-Sector Catalyst Drift Validation V1

**Status:** FROZEN FOR FIRST-PASS VALIDATION  
**Protocol version:** v1.0  
**Frozen date:** 2026-08-23  
**Research type:** cross-sector event study / strategy-selection gate  
**Primary purpose:** determine whether complex, decision-relevant corporate catalysts exhibit economically meaningful post-initial-repricing drift over the next 1–5 trading days.

---

## 1. Research question

The only primary question for V1 is:

> After a major, verifiable and economically meaningful corporate catalyst becomes public and the first 30 minutes of executable price discovery have passed, is there still a stable, tradable abnormal return over the next 1, 3 and 5 trading days?

Formally, estimate the distribution of:

- raw return from the defined entry time to T+1D / T+3D / T+5D;
- SPY-adjusted excess return over the same horizons;
- sector-adjusted excess return where a valid sector benchmark exists;
- MFE and MAE over the 1D / 3D / 5D horizons.

This protocol is deliberately **not** an intraday-pattern study. It is a strategy-selection gate for deciding whether further research into catalyst-aware execution is justified.

---

## 2. Anti-bias objective

This study must not be driven by the Moderna / biotech case.

Rules:

1. The study universe is cross-sector.
2. No single sector may exceed 25% of the final accepted event sample.
3. No single event category may exceed 35% of the final accepted event sample.
4. No single issuer may contribute more than 2 accepted events in V1 unless the second event is economically independent and separated by at least 180 calendar days.
5. MRNA may be included only as one ordinary sample observation if it satisfies all frozen inclusion rules.
6. Event selection must not depend on subsequent stock performance.

---

## 3. Event taxonomy

Each event must be assigned to exactly one primary category before outcome calculation.

### E1 — Clinical / Regulatory
Examples: pivotal clinical readout, FDA approval/CRL, clinical hold, major label expansion, material regulatory decision.

### E2 — Earnings / Guidance Reset
Not every earnings release qualifies. The event must contain a material change in forward economics such as guidance reset, profitability inflection, margin reset, major estimate revision or major business-model change.

### E3 — Major Contract / Order / Capacity
Examples: economically material customer contract, backlog/order step-change, capacity agreement, hyperscaler agreement, major defense/industrial award.

### E4 — Product / Commercialization / Strategic Change
Examples: material new product launch, commercialization inflection, platform validation, strategic reorganization, major partnership with direct economic consequences.

### E5 — M&A / Policy / Supply-Chain Shock
Examples: merger/acquisition outcome, material policy decision, tariff/export-control change, license decision, supply disruption or strategic raw-material change with direct company economics.

Ambiguous events must be rejected or assigned using information available at the event timestamp only.

---

## 4. Eligible security universe

Primary universe:

- U.S.-listed common stocks with executable QuantConnect equity data;
- NYSE / Nasdaq / NYSE American common equities where available;
- price at event-entry timestamp >= USD 5;
- prior 20-trading-day median daily dollar volume >= USD 20 million;
- ordinary operating companies.

Exclude:

- ETFs, ETNs, closed-end funds;
- SPAC shells and pre-combination blank-check companies;
- OTC securities;
- securities with unusable or missing entry-price data;
- events where trading is halted through the complete intended entry window and no objective executable entry can be defined;
- events with unresolved symbol/corporate-action ambiguity.

Liquidity thresholds are screening rules, not alpha variables in V1.

---

## 5. Point-in-time integrity

PIT integrity is mandatory.

For every accepted event, store:

- ticker / permanent identifier where available;
- issuer name;
- event category;
- event date;
- exact public release timestamp or best verified timestamp;
- timestamp timezone;
- source URL / source identity;
- whether the event occurred pre-market, regular session, after-hours or non-trading day;
- evidence that the information was public by the recorded timestamp;
- event-summary text limited to facts publicly available at that time.

Forbidden:

- using later revised press releases to classify an earlier timestamp;
- selecting the event because the subsequent move was large;
- using future analyst notes, revised guidance interpretation or later conference commentary to upgrade event importance retroactively;
- defining entry zones using future highs/lows.

If release time cannot be verified sufficiently for the entry rule, reject the event from the primary sample and retain it only in an audit-exclusion table.

---

## 6. Entry timestamp definition

The main specification is intentionally simple.

### Regular-session release
If the event first becomes public during regular trading hours:

`entry_time = first tradable timestamp at or after event_timestamp + 30 minutes`

Use the first available minute bar at or after that timestamp.

### Pre-market release
If first public before the regular session open:

`entry_time = 10:00:00 America/New_York on the same trading day`

### After-hours / overnight / non-trading-day release
If first public after the regular close or on a non-trading day:

`entry_time = 10:00:00 America/New_York on the next regular trading day`

### Halt handling
If the stock is halted at the scheduled entry time, entry is the first objectively tradable minute after reopening. Flag `halt_adjusted_entry = true`.

No ORB, VWAP, pullback, reclaim, support/resistance, path-efficiency or trend-confirmation condition is allowed in V1 entry selection.

---

## 7. Return horizons and labels

For each event, calculate from entry price:

- `R_1D`: close of the first full regular trading day after entry versus entry;
- `R_3D`: close three full regular trading days after entry versus entry;
- `R_5D`: close five full regular trading days after entry versus entry.

If entry occurs before the same-day close, that partial day is Day 0. T+1 means the next full close after entry.

Also calculate:

- `SPY_XR_1D`, `SPY_XR_3D`, `SPY_XR_5D`;
- `SECTOR_XR_1D`, `SECTOR_XR_3D`, `SECTOR_XR_5D` when a frozen sector ETF mapping is available;
- MFE and MAE from entry through each horizon;
- time-to-MFE and time-to-MAE where minute data permit.

Primary decision horizon is **3D and 5D**. 1D is diagnostic.

---

## 8. Benchmark definition

### Market benchmark
SPY is the mandatory market benchmark.

### Sector benchmark
Use a frozen one-to-one sector mapping defined before results are inspected. If no reliable benchmark exists, mark sector-adjusted return missing rather than improvising.

Sector adjustment is secondary to SPY adjustment in V1.

## 10. Allowed explanatory variables in V1

Only a deliberately small set may be used for descriptive stratification:

- event category;
- sector;
- market-cap bucket measured PIT;
- release-session type;
- opening / entry gap versus previous close;
- RVOL measured using information available by entry time;
- prior 20-day median dollar volume.

These variables are not entry filters in the primary specification unless explicitly stated in Sections 4–6.

Not allowed in V1 model selection:

- RSI;
- MACD;
- moving-average crossovers;
- support/resistance;
- VWAP acceptance;
- opening-range breakout;
- higher-low / lower-high labels;
- path efficiency;
- first pullback / retest;
- short-interest thresholds;
- option-flow / dealer-gamma inference;
- LLM catalyst-quality scores;
- discretionary chart labels.

These are deferred until the primary drift hypothesis survives the strategy-selection gate.

## 11. Sample construction target

Target primary sample:

- minimum: 100 accepted events;
- preferred first pass: 125–150 accepted events;
- target period: 2018-01-01 through 2025-12-31;
- if minute-data or timestamp coverage is inadequate, the start date may move forward only once, before outcome inspection, and must be documented as a protocol amendment.

Preferred balance:

- approximately 20–35 accepted events per event category where feasible;
- sector concentration <= 25%;
- category concentration <= 35%.

Do not force equal cell counts by adding weak or unverifiable events.

---

## 12. Cost model

Report three views:

1. **Gross:** no execution cost.
2. **Baseline cost:** 25 bps round trip deducted from each completed event trade.
3. **Stress cost:** 50 bps round trip deducted from each completed event trade.

These are strategy-selection assumptions, not claims of exact realized cost. Events with obvious halt / spread / liquidity abnormalities must also be flagged separately.

If later live execution evidence supports a different model, that belongs to a later protocol version and must not rewrite V1 results.

---

## 13. Mandatory statistics

For each horizon, report:

- N;
- mean raw return;
- median raw return;
- mean SPY-adjusted return;
- median SPY-adjusted return;
- positive-return share;
- positive-SPY-excess-return share;
- standard deviation;
- 5th / 25th / 75th / 95th percentiles;
- bootstrap 95% confidence interval for the mean and median;
- average MFE;
- average MAE;
- baseline-cost and stress-cost adjusted mean / median.

Also report the same summary by event category, but V1 must not optimize rules from these subgroups.

Issuer-clustered or event-cluster-aware uncertainty should be used where feasible. If not feasible in the first pass, mark this as a limitation rather than ignoring dependence.

---

## 14. Tail and concentration audit

To prevent a few MRNA-like outcomes from creating a false strategy:

Mandatory diagnostics:

- contribution of the top 1, top 5 and top 10 events to aggregate strategy P&L;
- winsorized mean at 2.5% / 97.5%;
- mean excluding the top 5 winners;
- median return;
- category-level contribution to aggregate P&L;
- sector-level contribution to aggregate P&L.

A strategy whose positive result disappears after excluding a small number of extreme winners must not be called broadly validated.

---

## 15. Frozen GO / NO-GO decision gate

The purpose of V1 is not to maximize Sharpe. It is to decide whether further catalyst-specific research deserves time.

### GO
Proceed to M6.2-P2 only if all mandatory integrity checks pass and **at least 3 of the following 4 conditions** hold:

1. **Economic effect:** at either 3D or 5D, mean SPY-adjusted return after the 50 bps stress cost is >= +0.50%.
2. **Distribution breadth:** at the same qualifying horizon, median SPY-adjusted return after the 25 bps baseline cost is > 0.
3. **Cross-category breadth:** at least 3 event categories have positive mean SPY-adjusted return at the qualifying horizon, and at least 2 of them have positive medians.
4. **Tail robustness:** after removing the top 5 winning events, the qualifying-horizon mean SPY-adjusted return remains > 0.

Additionally, the bootstrap confidence interval and sample diagnostics must not indicate that the apparent effect is solely an artifact of one sector, one issuer or one extreme tail cluster.

### CONDITIONAL GO
If exactly 2 of 4 conditions hold, do not build an execution strategy. Permit one narrow follow-up whose sole purpose is to determine whether a clearly defined event subclass drives the effect. No intraday-state research is permitted yet.

### NO-GO
If 0–1 of 4 conditions hold, or PIT/data integrity is inadequate:

- stop the catalyst-drift strategy-development branch;
- do not proceed to PROBE / CONFIRM / path-state optimization;
- retain the result as a negative case in the research library.

No threshold may be relaxed after seeing the first-pass outcome.

---

## 16. What V1 can and cannot conclude

If GO:

V1 supports the statement that a cross-sector catalyst-drift effect is sufficiently promising to justify deeper testing. It does **not** establish a production alpha, optimal entry, sizing rule or live trading strategy.

If NO-GO:

V1 rejects further broad cross-sector catalyst-drift development under this specification. It does not prove that all event-driven strategies are invalid.

---

## 17. Deferred hypotheses

Only after GO may later work test incremental value from:

- catalyst quality / semantic complexity;
- expectation gap;
- information completeness;
- RVOL as an interaction variable;
- estimate-revision dynamics;
- PROBE versus delayed confirmation entry;
- ORB / VWAP / pullback state;
- path efficiency;
- short interest / option positioning;
- portfolio construction and execution optimization.

Each added layer must demonstrate incremental out-of-sample value versus the simpler baseline.
